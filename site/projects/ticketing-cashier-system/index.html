Case study

Ticketing & Cashier System

QR ticketing for a venue with turnstile entry gates: cashier counters that sell and print tickets on thermal printers, gates that check each code against the database, and managers who see occupancy, sales and cash reconciliation live. Everything runs on an on-site server, so selling and entry carry on without the internet.

The problem

A busy venue needed to sell entry tickets at several counters and let visitors through tripod turnstiles by scanning a QR code. The queue at the counter has to move fast, an unpaid ticket must never open a gate, and cash has to reconcile at the end of every shift, even though no supervisor stands at each counter to approve refunds and reprints.

The venue's internet connection can't be trusted, and a gate that stops opening on a busy afternoon is not an option. So the system has to keep selling, and keep letting people in, when a counter, the server or the network drops.

Approach

Print first, then charge

The cashier picks ticket types and presses one key: the order is created and every ticket prints at once, but the tickets stay inactive until the payment is confirmed. The visitor walks away while the cashier counts change, and a ticket printed for someone who then didn't pay is cancelled automatically and rejected at the gate.

Rules in the database, not the counter

Selling, payment, validation and every ticket action are PostgreSQL functions, so each rule is enforced atomically in one place. Cashiers read money tables only through functions; the gate service is a separate database role that can only validate tickets and report its heartbeat. Gate validation has a budget of 10 ms at the 95th percentile, backed by pgTAP tests and volume tests at a year's worth of tickets.

Controls without a supervisor at every counter

Reprints, reactivations (scanned but didn't pass) and void-refunds need no approval, but each one needs a reason, stays within limits the database enforces, and lands in an actions log. Automatic review flags pick out the patterns worth a look: "other" as a reason, too many actions in one shift, a ticket printed too many times, a reactivated ticket that then entered through a different gate.

A blind cash count

The cashier never sees expected cash. Not on the sell screen, not at close, not on the printed shift receipt. They count each denomination, and a supervisor reconciles expected against counted on the admin side. Nobody approves their own shift, and a difference needs a written comment.

Selling through an outage

While online, each counter reserves a pool of random gate codes on the server. When the health check fails, the counter switches to charge-first-then-print and sells from its pool, so two counters can never issue the same code. Each counter's tickets and codes are also held on the controller of its home gate, so during an outage a ticket opens its home gate. On reconnect the queue uploads in batches, idempotently, keeping the original sale times. Anything the server refuses is flagged for a supervisor rather than lost.

The hard parts

Arabic on a thermal printer. ESC/POS code pages can't shape Arabic text. Tickets and shift receipts are laid out as Flutter widgets, rendered off-screen and sent to the printer as raster images through hand-written ESC/POS commands. The QR code is painted on whole printer dots so it stays sharp after the black-and-white cut.

A dead server that never says so. A server that accepts a request and never answers left the payment screen spinning. Every server call now times out, and once the health check agrees the server is gone, the cashier can go back to selling offline while the unpaid-order job cleans up the abandoned order.

What "today" means after midnight. A closing time of 02:00 at first refused every sale, because 02:00 meant that morning. One venue-day definition, shared by the database, the offline counter and every report, fixed it everywhere at once.

Try it

The demo is the real cashier and admin app. Its database is swapped for an in-memory store that answers the same functions with the same rules and error codes. The bar at the bottom stands in for the hardware a browser doesn't have:

  • View as the cashier, the supervisor or the admin.
  • Printer tray shows every ticket and receipt as it would print.
  • Gate scanner holds a code to a turnstile: try a ticket before and after it's paid.
  • Server outage takes the server away. Keep selling, then switch it back and watch the queue upload.
  • Jam printer makes printing fail, to see the retry and "leave unprinted" paths.

A good first run: open a shift as the cashier, sell two tickets, take a cash payment with a tip, scan one ticket twice at the gate, then switch to the supervisor and reconcile the shift that's waiting for approval.
